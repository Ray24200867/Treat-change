新春開心果糯米糍 ｜ 沒有角色之分，只有一起學習

2026年2月13日

【CNY Pistachio Mochi】No Fixed Roles, Just Learning Together

上星期日，我哋聯同香港西區扶輪社匡智晨輝學校，15 位不同能力嘅同學，夥拍 15 位來自華懋集團 @chinachemgroup 嘅義工，一齊完成咗一場又甜又暖嘅開心果糯米糍體驗。

呢一日，大部分義工都係第一次接觸有特殊學習需要嘅同學，第一次見面就已經一齊落手搓糯米糍。有同學同義工一齊，將原本應該係熊貓造型嘅糯米糍，創作成史路比公仔；亦有同學主動教返本身冇乜煮食經驗嘅義工點樣搓麵粉。短短兩個小時入面，義工同同學並肩合作，冇「教」同「被教」之分，只有一齊試、一齊學。每個人都可以係學習者，亦可以係引導者。

分享環節入面，義工形容同學溫暖、真誠。有義工分享自己個拍檔仔好願意讚美別人，原來呢位同學當日行勻全場，逐個為大家送上鼓勵。呢份平等互動嘅經驗，令義工對「共融」有咗全新體會「原來共融係能夠取長補短，互相理解，同心合作。」有義工參與今次活動後更話「希望社會上的人多些機會接觸不同能力的人，亦讓不同能力人士有多些機會接觸社會。 」

Last Sunday, we partnered with Rotary Club of Hong Kong Island West Hong Chi Morninghope School, bringing together 15 students of different abilities and 15 volunteers from Chinachem Group for a sweet and heartwarming pistachio mochi-making experience.

For many of the volunteers, it was their first time meeting students with special needs. From the very first moment, there were no labels or expectations — just everyone rolling up their sleeves and making mochi together. One group turned what was meant to be a panda-shaped mochi into a Snoopy character, while some students took the lead in teaching volunteers, who had little cooking experience, how to knead the dough properly.

Within just two hours, volunteers and students worked side by side. There was no “teacher” or “learner” — only trying, learning, and exploring together. Roles shifted naturally, and everyone had the chance to learn from one another, and to guide one another in return.

During the sharing session, volunteers described the students as warm and genuine. One volunteer shared that their student partner loved encouraging others — and in fact, that student had walked around the room, offering compliments and encouragement to everyone present.

This experience of equal interaction reshaped how volunteers understood inclusion. As one volunteer reflected, “Inclusion is about complementing one another, understanding each other, and working together as a team.” Another volunteer shared a hope that more people in society would have opportunities to meet people of different abilities — and that people of different abilities would also have more chances to engage with the wider community.
